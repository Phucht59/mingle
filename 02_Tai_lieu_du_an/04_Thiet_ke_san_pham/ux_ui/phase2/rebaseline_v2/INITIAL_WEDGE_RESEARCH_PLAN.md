## Research question and circumstances

Ask: **When someone intends to improve usable English in a specific circumstance, what progress are they trying to make, what do they use today, and what prevents that progress?** Do not begin with “Would you like an adaptive app?” or a demographic persona that already assumes the answer.

Use [Jobs-to-Be-Done](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/) to investigate circumstances and desired progress, and [Working Backwards](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/an-insider-look-at-amazons-culture-and-processes) to state a customer problem before committing to features. These are discovery methods, not evidence that a particular wedge exists.

Candidate circumstances to explore, not frozen segments: restarting after previous abandonment; preparing for a near-term everyday/work interaction; fitting practice around interruptions; choosing between app/content/cards/tutor/manual study/no action; needing available content when network is unreliable. Ask participants for a recent actual case and examine exceptions. Do not infer need from occupation, test score, phone model or a label such as “lazy learner.”

## Recruitment criteria and ethical preparation

Proposed first round: approximately five adult participants, Vietnamese-speaking where appropriate to the current interface, with a recent self-chosen English learning goal or recent abandoned attempt. Vary current alternatives, familiarity with mobile learning, time/connectivity constraints and accessibility needs when feasible. Android-first use is a platform context, not proof of a target market. A convenience sample cannot establish prevalence, a whole demographic segment or saturation.

Do not recruit only coworkers or enthusiasts already coached on Mingo. Record recruitment channel and relationship so bias is visible. Adult participation is a conservative scope for this exploratory protocol, not a final product age decision. Recruitment/consent/contact handling, recording permissions, retention/access and compensation if any require a real organizer's approval before recruitment. Participation is voluntary; no grades, employer evaluation, school/medical status or sensitive demographic profiling. Collect coded participant IDs; store consent/contact separately; allow withdrawal/redaction within the agreed procedure. No consent can be inferred from app use.

## JTBD interview script — 30–40 minutes

Use neutral prompts; follow the person's timeline. Do not supply favorable vocabulary or explain the proposed product before the past-behavior section.

| Topic | Moderator prompt | Evidence to retain |
|---|---|---|
| Recent actual case | “Kể lần gần nhất bạn muốn học hoặc dùng tiếng Anh. Chuyện gì đã xảy ra?” | Date range/circumstance in their words; actual intended outcome |
| Trigger / why now | “Điều gì khiến bạn bắt đầu lúc đó? Trước đó bạn làm gì?” | Trigger, urgency, constraints; no assumed motivation label |
| Desired progress | “Nếu việc đó tốt hơn, bạn sẽ làm được điều gì cụ thể?” | Observable outcome and context; functional/social/emotional goals only as reported |
| Current alternative | “Bạn đã dùng cách nào? Cho tôi xem một ví dụ nếu bạn muốn.” | Actual tool/manual/tutor/no-action choice; optional redacted artifact, no private credentials |
| Decision / tradeoff | “Bạn cân nhắc những cách nào? Vì sao chọn cách này?” | Alternatives, cost/effort/trust constraints and what was rejected |
| Actual behavior | “Lần đó bạn mở gì, làm gì, dừng ở đâu? Sau đó thì sao?” | Sequence and interruption, remembered vs demonstrated behavior clearly separated |
| Friction | “Phần nào khó hoặc không đáng công? Có lần nào lại thuận lợi?” | Concrete pain, successful counterexample, technical/content/context explanations |
| Abandonment | “Bạn có lần ngừng học hoặc đổi cách không? Điều gì xảy ra ngay trước đó?” | Circumstances and alternatives; no leading guilt/streak explanation |
| Return / no return | “Điều gì khiến bạn quay lại? Khi không quay lại, bạn làm gì thay thế?” | Real choice, prompts, external instruction, competing progress |
| Willingness to switch | “Muốn đổi cách học, bạn cần điều gì? Lần gần nhất bạn thật sự đổi là khi nào?” | Previous switch/action, switching cost, anxieties/habits; hypothetical willingness is weak evidence |
| Success / trust | “Bạn biết mình tiến bộ bằng cách nào? Kết quả nào bạn tin, vì sao?” | Evidence they use today; mismatch with app counts or pressure |
| Close / disconfirm | “Điều gì khiến một công cụ mới không phù hợp với bạn? Tôi hiểu sai điều gì?” | Explicit objections, cases outside the proposed problem |

If a Mingo concept is shown after this interview, mark reactions as concept responses, separate from past behavior. “I would use it” is not adoption or willingness to pay. Do not make a sale, enroll an account or contact another person as part of this task.

## Working Backwards exercise — explicitly hypothetical

Use this blank mini PR/FAQ after interviews; preserve the evidence IDs behind each sentence:

“For **[circumstance identified from actual cases]**, Mingo helps **[observable desired progress]** with **[smallest supported learning action]**. Unlike **[actual alternative/tradeoff]**, the candidate offers **[difference to validate]**. The supporting evidence is **[case/task/learning observation]**, and the remaining uncertainty is **[counterexample/missing outcome]**.”

FAQ: Who has this problem and when? What do they do today? Why is today inadequate? What evidence could disprove the problem? What is the smallest useful core loop? How will meaningful progress be checked later? What should remain outside scope? Why would someone switch without pressure? Which privacy/offline/accessibility costs are acceptable?
