# Product Vision and Positioning

## Product identity

The system should feel less like a static course catalog and more like a **learning companion** that helps the learner know what to do next, keeps sessions lightweight, and can intervene early when learning risk appears.

## Learner-side product direction retained from project history

The following ideas have been explicitly discussed and should be treated as product constraints/hypotheses until validated:

- Default learning sessions should be **short and easy to start**, with ~5 minutes used as a design anchor.
- The 5-minute target is not itself claimed as pedagogically optimal. Its purpose is to match modern content-consumption behavior while preserving real learning value.
- The system should include retention/motivation mechanics such as:
  - streaks;
  - rewards;
  - learner targets/goals.
- The experience should feel like a **friend/companion for studying**, not merely an admin-driven LMS.
- Content sourcing is expected to rely heavily on **public/legitimate learning sources**, because the project is being built by a small team/individual and is not staffed by a full curriculum organization.
- A small pilot of roughly **5 real users** was discussed as a realistic early testing size under resource constraints.

## Important interpretation

These are not immutable pedagogical truths. The implementation should distinguish:

- product hypotheses;
- evidence-backed learning principles;
- implementation contracts.

The product should avoid turning short sessions, streaks, or gamification into a substitute for learning quality.
